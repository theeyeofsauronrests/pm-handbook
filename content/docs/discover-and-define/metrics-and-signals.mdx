---
title: Metrics and Signals
description: How PMs use metrics to inform judgment without turning dashboards into proof.
---

Metrics exist to inform decisions, not to validate them after the fact.

A good KPI answers: **What should we pay attention to right now?**

A bad KPI answers: **How do we prove we were right?**

## Outputs and outcomes

Outputs describe what was delivered: features shipped, tickets closed, deployments completed, documents written.

Outcomes describe what changed: user behavior, workflow quality, risk, reliability, cost, trust, or adoption.

Outputs are necessary. Outcomes are what matter.

## Leading and lagging indicators

Lagging indicators confirm what already happened: adoption, performance, cost, incidents, user impact, or missed deadlines.

Leading indicators suggest what may happen next: usage patterns, error rates, support volume, workflow friction, stale tickets, dependency churn, or repeated exceptions.

PMs should favor leading indicators when making decisions and lagging indicators when evaluating impact.

## Good KPIs have jobs

Good KPIs are:

- Directional, not falsely precise
- Hard to game
- Tied to a decision or action
- Interpretable by the team
- Useful when they move

Bad KPIs:

- Become performance management in disguise
- Create incentives to hide problems
- Replace judgment with dashboards
- End the conversation instead of improving it

## Metrics are conversation starters

Useful metrics prompt better questions:

- Why is this changing?
- What assumption might be wrong?
- What would we try next?
- What signal would make us stop?
- Is the metric measuring the behavior we actually care about?

If a metric ends the conversation, it is probably being misused.

## Bottom line

Metrics should make uncertainty more visible. They should not create a comforting illusion that the product is understood.

<PMTakeaway>
Pick metrics by the decisions they support. If nobody knows what would change when the number moves, the metric is decoration.
</PMTakeaway>
