This handbook is a compact field guide for B2B Product Managers working alongside Design and Engineering in regulated or compliance-heavy industries.

It is guidance, not policy. Use it to make better decisions about scope, tradeoffs, clarity, reliability, and delivery.

It is based on Shane Quinlan's experience in startups and scale-ups across cyber, compliance, healthcare, defense, and fintech.

It is not a B2C growth playbook. It is not written for a MANGA-scale company with armies of specialized PMs, researchers, data scientists, program managers, and platform teams. Those worlds have their own physics.

This handbook is for smaller organizations where the PM is often closer to the customer, closer to the delivery team, closer to the politics, and more exposed when tradeoffs are vague.

It is also incomplete.

Shane has had to rewrite versions of this kind of handbook a half-dozen times. This version is an attempt to make the useful parts more portable and longer-lasting: less tied to one company, one process, or one moment in the tooling cycle.

Expect it to change. The goal is not to finish the subject. The goal is to keep the judgment useful.

## How to read it

Read the page closest to the decision in front of you. This is not a course and it does not need to be read linearly.

- Use the left navigation when you know the topic.
- Use search when you know the term.
- Follow previous and next links when you want the surrounding context.

## What this handbook values

- Clear problem framing
- Metrics that inform decisions instead of proving narratives
- Clear build, reuse, buy, simplify, or stop decisions
- Restraint where complexity would create risk
- Tickets that coordinate work without pretending to replace conversation
- Engineering signals, including cloud-cost behavior, that PMs can read without pretending to be engineers
- Skepticism toward hype, especially around “AI”
- Decision tenets that make repeated tradeoffs explicit

## What it is not

It is not a corporate policy portal, certification course, B2C growth manual, big-tech operating model, or generic encyclopedia of product frameworks.

The handbook should help PMs ask sharper questions and make tradeoffs visible. It should not create process theater.

For recurring product tradeoffs, see [Decision Tenets](/docs/reference/decision-tenets).
