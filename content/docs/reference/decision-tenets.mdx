---
title: Decision Tenets
description: Recurring product judgment rules for tradeoffs, uncertainty, and delivery.
---

Decision tenets are not slogans. They are rules of thumb that help teams stop relitigating the same tradeoffs.

A good tenet is specific enough that a reasonable person could argue the opposite.

## Prefer clarity over breadth

A smaller product that behaves predictably is often better than a broader product full of exceptions.

Breadth creates surface area. Surface area creates support burden, test burden, design burden, and user confusion.

## Choose the starting point deliberately

Build, reuse, buy, simplify, or stop. Each option can be right.

The PM's job is to make the tradeoff visible: what the team gains, what it inherits, how hard the choice is to reverse, and who carries the cost later.

## Speed matters when learning moves

Fast artifacts are useful when they produce faster decisions.

If the team builds quickly but does not reorient quickly, the bottleneck is not production speed. It is learning, alignment, or courage.

## Move faster on reversible decisions

Do not give every decision the same ceremony.

Spend more time on decisions that are hard to reverse, affect security or reliability, create long-lived dependencies, or constrain other teams. Move faster when the team can learn and adjust.

## Cut scope before quality

When constraint appears, reduce what the product does before reducing whether it works.

Incomplete scope is negotiable. Unreliable behavior erodes trust.

## Artifacts should change decisions

A ticket, roadmap, Blog / FAQ, prototype, or PRD is useful when it changes what the team understands or does next.

If an artifact does not expose a decision, assumption, risk, or tradeoff, it may be theater.

## Delete stale work

Backlog items, features, and old assumptions all carry cost.

If the team would not start the work today with current knowledge, it should be reframed, archived, or killed.

<PMTakeaway>
Use tenets to make recurring tradeoffs faster and more explicit. Do not use them to avoid thinking.
</PMTakeaway>
