This glossary defines handbook terms in plain language. It is not a standards document.

## Blog / FAQ

A lightweight framing artifact for a feature, program, project, or contract. It explains what is being built, why it matters, how the team expects to approach it, and what questions need answers.

## Composition

Building a product from multiple parts: new work, existing tools, services, interfaces, patterns, or commercial products.

## Build / reuse / buy decision

A decision about the best starting point for a product need. The answer may be new work, an existing capability, a third-party product, a simpler process change, or no work at all.

## Decision trigger

A signal that causes the team to continue, stop, pivot, promote, or reframe work.

## Fan-out

The number of consumers reading from the same data stream, API, event feed, or service. More fan-out often means more load, coordination, and cost.

## Feature

A coherent user-facing capability that delivers recognizable value. A feature should connect to a user behavior, business decision, risk reduction, cost change, or measurable outcome.

## Epic

A temporary planning wrapper for related work that is too large to manage as one ticket. A useful epic explains the outcome, scope boundary, risks, dependencies, and decision it supports.

## Theme

A direction of investment used to compare bodies of work that may not fit under one feature. A good theme is specific enough to say no.

## Initiative

A larger commitment around a strategic outcome. An initiative should have an owner, opportunity cost, known tradeoffs, and a clear explanation of what will not be pursued while it is active.

## KPI

A key performance indicator. In this handbook, a KPI is useful only when it informs a decision or helps the team notice a meaningful change.

## Leading indicator

A signal that suggests what may happen next, such as increasing error rates, support volume, workflow friction, or cost growth.

## Lagging indicator

A signal that confirms what already happened, such as adoption, incident count, spend, missed deadlines, or measured impact.

## Retention

How long data is kept before deletion, aggregation, or archival. Retention is a product decision when it affects user value, compliance, cost, or system behavior.

## Roadmap

A communication model for priorities, sequencing, confidence, dependencies, and uncertainty. It is not a contract with the future.

## Production-bound artifact

An artifact intended to move toward real delivery, not just a demo. It needs an owner, review path, constraints, validation, and an integration destination.

## Product Owner

A [Scrum accountability](https://www.scrum.org/resources/what-is-a-product-owner) focused on product value and effective backlog management. Product Owner responsibilities may sit inside Product Management, but they are not the whole Product Manager role.

## Product Trio

The cross-functional leadership pattern of Product, Design, and Engineering working together to address viability, usability, feasibility, and product quality.

## Product restraint

The discipline of choosing fewer, clearer capabilities so the product can remain understandable, reliable, and maintainable.

## Scrum Master

A [Scrum accountability](https://www.scrum.org/resources/accountability-responsibility-and-roles) focused on team effectiveness and Scrum practice. The useful work is helping the team inspect, adapt, coordinate, and avoid overcommitment. The failure mode is process policing.

## PR / MR

A Pull Request or Merge Request: a proposed code change waiting to be reviewed and integrated into a shared codebase.

## Reuse

Using an existing capability instead of creating a new local solution. Reuse can help, but it also creates constraints and dependency cost.

## SemVer

Semantic Versioning. A release convention using `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH` to communicate expected compatibility and change impact.

## Data service

A service or interface for accessing, transforming, or coordinating data.

## Design system

A set of interface components, patterns, and design decisions that help products stay coherent.

## Spike

A time-boxed learning activity. A spike should answer a specific question and produce a decision, not just research notes.

## Changesets

A tool and workflow for documenting package changes, choosing version impact, and generating release notes near the code.
