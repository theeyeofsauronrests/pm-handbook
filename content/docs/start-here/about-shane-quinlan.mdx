Shane Quinlan writes this handbook from experience in B2B Product Management across startups and scale-ups in cyber, compliance, healthcare, defense, and fintech.

The point of view is personal. It is not a neutral survey of product frameworks, and it is not trying to represent one company, certification path, or operating model. The handbook reflects the kind of product work where PMs are close to customers, close to delivery, close to organizational politics, and accountable for making tradeoffs visible before they become expensive.

Shane's product writing tends to return to a few themes:

- Product Management is judgment under constraint.
- Politics is part of the job.
- Artifacts are useful only when they improve decisions.
- Process theater is still theater, even when the template is well formatted.
- PMs should understand enough of Design and Engineering to collaborate honestly without pretending to own every discipline.
- AI can help organize and pressure-test thinking, but it cannot own intent, risk, impact, or accountability.
