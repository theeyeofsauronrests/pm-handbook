---
title: How to Use This Handbook
description: How to use the handbook as decision support without turning it into process theater.
---

Use this handbook when a product decision needs sharper framing.

It is not meant to be read like a course or enforced like a policy. Read the page closest to the decision in front of you, then use it to ask better questions.

## Who it is for

This handbook is written from the perspective of B2B Product Management in regulated or compliance-heavy industries, especially in smaller organizations where Product, Design, and Engineering work close to the actual mess.

That means startups and scale-ups more than giant consumer platforms. It draws from cyber, compliance, healthcare, defense, and fintech work, but the recommendations are not industry rituals. They are patterns for PMs who have to make tradeoffs with incomplete information, real customers, constrained teams, and organizational politics.

It is not a B2C growth playbook. It is not a mature big-tech operating model. If you have a large research org, a growth experimentation machine, and a dozen platform teams behind you, some of this will still apply, but the pressure points are different.

## When to use it

Use the handbook before or during:

- Discovery and framing conversations
- Blog / FAQ drafting
- Backlog cleanup
- Design critique
- Engineering handoff
- Dependency decisions
- AI-assisted prototyping
- Delivery reviews

The goal is not to quote the handbook. The goal is to make the decision clearer.

## How to read a page

Most pages are short by design.

Read for:

- The decision the page helps with
- The tradeoffs it names
- The questions it suggests
- The failure modes it warns about
- The related page you should read next

If a page does not change what the team does next, treat that as a signal. The page may need improvement, or the problem may not be the one the team thought it was.

## Do not weaponize it

This handbook should not become a way to win arguments by citation.

Use it to clarify:

- What is known
- What is assumed
- Who owns the decision
- What would change the plan
- What the team is choosing not to do

If the handbook makes a conversation less honest, it is being used wrong.

## Bottom line

Use the handbook as decision support. Keep the judgment in the team.
