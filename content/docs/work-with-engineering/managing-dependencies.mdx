title: Managing Dependencies
description: How PMs account for the leverage and cost of external, internal, and third-party software.
---

Dependencies are part of the product operating model.

We assemble products from open-source libraries, internal libraries, platform services, third-party products, runtimes, and build tools. A dependency may solve a large problem quickly, but it also creates work that must be accounted for.

## Dependencies are not free

When a library is proposed, ask:

- What problem does it solve?
- Is that problem already solved by an existing tool, service, or product?
- Who maintains it?
- How active is the project?
- What are its security and licensing implications?
- What other dependencies does it introduce?
- What will upgrading or replacing it require?
- Are we willing to own it for the life of the product?

The question is not only, “Can we use this?”

It is, “Are we willing to carry this?”

## The dependency chain

Products depend on libraries, services, tools, and platforms. Those dependencies have dependencies of their own.

On the frontend, the chain may include:

- Node.js
- TypeScript
- React
- Next.js
- deck.gl
- UI, state, testing, build, linting, and formatting packages

Tooling dependencies such as Biome may not ship with the application, but they still affect development, CI, upgrades, and contributor workflow.

## PM responsibilities

PMs do not manage package files line by line. They help make dependency decisions visible and intentional.

That includes:

- Treating major upgrades as planned product work
- Coordinating changes across consumers
- Accounting for maintenance and migration costs
- Resisting one-off forks that create unsupported local ownership
- Understanding the dependencies the product consumes

## Bottom line

Dependencies are decisions with carrying cost. PMs should make that cost visible before it becomes invisible product debt.

<PMTakeaway>
The question is not whether a dependency accelerates the first version. It is whether the product can afford the ownership it creates.
</PMTakeaway>
