---
title: Reading PRs and MRs
description: How PMs use code-change proposals as signals for progress, scope, and risk.
---

Pull Requests on GitHub and Merge Requests on GitLab are proposals to integrate a discrete change into a shared codebase.

PMs are not expected to perform code review. They can still use PRs and MRs to understand intent, scope, progress, and delivery risk.

## Review quality is contextual

The amount of review varies with the reviewer, author, codebase, and risk of the change.

A senior maintainer may perform a deep architectural review. A junior reviewer may focus on narrower concerns. A trusted maintainer may approve a familiar, low-risk change quickly. Some reviews are unfortunately little more than rubber stamps.

A quiet review is therefore not automatically healthy or unhealthy.

The PM question is:

> Is the depth of review appropriate to the risk of the change?

## What to look for

### Intent

A healthy PR or MR has a descriptive title, a short explanation of why the change exists, and links to the relevant ticket or feature artifact.

### Scope

Small changes are usually easier to review and reverse. Large or long-running changes may indicate hidden complexity, dependencies, or unresolved decisions. Size alone is not bad; unexamined size is.

### Discussion

Look for meaningful questions, requested changes, unresolved threads, and periods of inactivity.

### Automation

Builds, tests, linters, and other checks should generally pass before merge. Automation does not guarantee correctness, but repeated failures are always a signal.

## Status is not impact

- Open does not necessarily mean blocked.
- Merged does not mean deployed.
- Deployed does not mean adopted.

PRs and MRs show engineering progress, not customer outcomes.
